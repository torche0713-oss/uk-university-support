Degree Apprenticeships UK: Complete Guide for Adults

Earn a degree while working — no tuition fees and a full salary

Degree apprenticeships are one of the best-kept secrets in UK higher education. You earn a full bachelor's or master's degree while working — with no tuition fees and a salary from day one. For mature students looking to change careers or upskill without taking on debt, they can be an excellent option.

This guide explains how degree apprenticeships work and whether they are right for you as an adult applicant.

What Is a Degree Apprenticeship?

A degree apprenticeship is a job with a fully integrated degree programme. You work for an employer, attend university part-time (usually one day per week or in blocks), and graduate with a full bachelor's or master's degree — all without paying tuition fees.

  • No tuition fees — the government and your employer cover the cost
  • You earn a salary throughout (£18,000 - £30,000+ depending on the role)
  • You gain 3-5 years of professional work experience alongside your degree
  • You graduate with a degree and a job, not just a qualification

Are Degree Apprenticeships Available for Adults?

Yes. Unlike traditional apprenticeships which are often associated with school-leavers, degree apprenticeships have no upper age limit. Many are designed for career changers and adult learners. Employers value mature applicants because of their work experience, professionalism, and commitment.

However, competition is strong. Adult applicants should emphasise their existing skills, career motivation, and why they want to switch into the field.

Popular Degree Apprenticeship Areas

  • Digital and Technology Solutions — IT, software engineering, data analytics, cybersecurity
  • Engineering and Manufacturing — aerospace, automotive, electrical, mechanical
  • Healthcare — nursing (adult, mental health, child), paramedic science
  • Business and Management — project management, leadership, business development
  • Finance and Accounting — chartered accountancy, investment management
  • Law — solicitor apprenticeships (including the SQE route)
  • Construction — architecture, quantity surveying, civil engineering

How Degree Apprenticeships Compare to Traditional Degrees

  • Cost: Degree apprenticeship = £0 tuition. Traditional degree = up to £9,535 per year
  • Income: Apprenticeship = salary from day one. Traditional degree = part-time work or maintenance loan
  • Duration: Apprenticeship = 3-5 years. Traditional degree = 3-4 years
  • Student life: Apprenticeship = mostly workplace-based. Traditional degree = campus-based
  • Outcome: Apprenticeship = degree + job experience. Traditional degree = degree + graduate job hunt

Entry Requirements for Adult Applicants

Entry requirements vary by programme and employer. Typical requirements include:

  • GCSEs in English and Maths (grade 4/C or above) — or equivalency tests
  • A-Levels, BTECs, or an Access to HE Diploma for Level 6 (bachelor's) apprenticeships
  • Relevant work experience (this is where mature students have an advantage)
  • Strong performance in the employer's assessment centre and interview

Tip for mature applicants: If you do not have recent formal qualifications, an Access to HE Diploma can help you meet the entry requirements for many degree apprenticeships. Your work experience is also a significant advantage — make sure to highlight it in your application.

How to Apply

  • Search for vacancies on the Gov.uk find an apprenticeship website
  • Check individual employer websites — many large companies advertise directly
  • Look at university apprenticeship pages — they list which employers they partner with
  • Prepare for assessment centres and interviews — employers assess both academic potential and workplace readiness
  • Apply early — popular programmes fill up quickly

Pros and Cons for Mature Students

Pros

  • No tuition fees — you avoid student debt entirely
  • Earn while you learn — full salary from day one
  • Relevant experience — 3-5 years of industry experience by graduation
  • Career change opportunity — many apprenticeships welcome career changers
  • No upper age limit — open to applicants of all ages

Cons

  • Competitive — fewer places available compared to traditional degree courses
  • Less flexibility — you commit to a specific employer and location
  • Intensive — balancing work and study requires strong time management
  • Salary may be lower than your current job — consider the financial transition carefully
